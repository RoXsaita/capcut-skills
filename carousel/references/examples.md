# Annotated examples

Two sets: the creator's own 22 Instagram carousels (June–October 2026), where we can see the
numbers, and patterns from other creators (see [research.md](research.md) for sources).

Numbers here are **relative**, so the file can stay public:

- **× median**: the carousel's reach divided by the median reach of the creator's 22 carousels.
- **% of reel**: the carousel's reach divided by the reach of the reel on the same topic posted
  just before it. This strips out account growth and the luck of the reel, and is the fairer
  comparison between carousels.
- **save %** and **share %**: saves or shares divided by reach.

Every one of the 22 had **6 slides**, so this data says nothing about slide count. That rule comes
from platform and creator evidence, not from these examples.

## The creator's winners

### 1. "Let Claude apply to LinkedIn jobs": 20× median, 18% of the reel
[instagram.com/p/DcoekgAlo3M](https://www.instagram.com/p/DcoekgAlo3M/) · save 5.6 % · share 5.2 %

- **The set-up was a reel the day before** that ended "want the prompt and the full walkthrough?
  comment «CV»", and drew roughly 6 comments per 100 people reached. The carousel is the thing
  that comment keyword promised. Its own last slide asks for a new keyword («برومبت»).
- **Cover in the second person, about the reader's outcome**: "let Claude apply to LinkedIn jobs
  (for you)", plus one line of the only requirement ("a Chrome extension"). Not "I let Claude…".
- **Numbered steps, one per slide**, each headline a verb: install, write one clear request,
  answer its questions first, if it can't find the CV let it search Gmail, review before sending.
- **A safety step** ("review before Submit, you approve") makes it feel honest, not hype.
- **Caption names the person to send it to**: "share it with someone who is applying for jobs".
  That is the strongest share target in the set (everyone knows someone job-hunting), and it has
  by far the highest share rate.
- What it would lose today: the UI is AI-drawn (an image model redrew the browser and the form),
  not the creator's real screen. It won despite that, not because of it.

### 2. "Hermes now gives free AI models": 9× median, 6% of a very big reel
[instagram.com/p/Dcb5fFEnAIj](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dcb5fFEnAIj/) · save 5.3 % · share 2.3 %

- Posted the day after a reel on the same news with roughly 20× the usual reel reach.
- **"Free" and "no API key, no account" on the cover**: the benefit and the removed obstacles.
- Steps 1–3 (download, open the app, run it free), then a **troubleshooting slide** ("models not
  showing? update Hermes"). Troubleshooting is what people save for later.
- Weak spots: slide 2 is a generic hub-and-spoke icon diagram, and the cover has a cartoon mascot.
  Both are AI-slop tells (see SKILL.md).

### 3. "Paying for AI? One of the strongest models in the world… free": 4× median, 48% of the reel
[instagram.com/p/Dc_pNqvlsjn](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dc_pNqvlsjn/) · save 4.7 % · share 1.7 %

The closest thing in the set to the target look. Read it slide by slide:

1. Cover: no image at all, a light paper background, a question in very large type, the payoff
   word ("free") in the accent, and a small line "swipe for the name and the method". The name
   is withheld, so the swipe has a reason.
2. **Real receipt**: the actual benchmark chart cropped to the bars that matter, the model's bar
   boxed, the number lifted into a label above it.
3. A second real chart, the same treatment.
4. The real pricing table, cropped, showing the free row.
5. Three numbered steps on one slide (download, connect, pick the free model), with one cropped
   real UI chip below.
6. **The honest catch**: "free has a price: they may train on your prompts. Don't paste secrets
   or client code." Then "save it and try it on a test project."

The reel it followed had only about the usual reel reach, so this carousel earned its reach on
its own. Receipts, a withheld name, and an honest caveat.

## The creator's losers, and the rule each one broke

### The recap: "Claude got me 30% back from IKEA": 0.7× median, 0.03% of the reel
[instagram.com/p/DbWOHzhirCu](https://www.instagram.com/p/DbWOHzhirCu/)

The reel the day before was the biggest the account has ever posted (nearly 3,000× this carousel's
reach). The carousel cover repeated the reel: the creator's face, "Claude got me 30% from IKEA,
$55 and I kept the chair". Everyone who cared had already seen that story. There was no new
promise for the reader, and the caption opened with a generic question ("got a product with a
problem?"). **Broke: cover promises the reader's outcome; carousel is the guide, not the recap.**

### Same mistake, smaller: "I let Claude cancel the subscription and get my money back": 1.7× median, 1.5% of the reel
[instagram.com/p/DcHKGSnFuvo](https://www.instagram.com/p/DcHKGSnFuvo/)

The reel asked for the keyword «دليل» (guide) and reached about 15× the usual reel. The carousel
did contain the guide and real-looking receipts, but its cover was first person ("I let Claude…"),
the story the viewer had already watched. **Broke: second-person cover.**

### Late, and the cover can't be read: "How AI agents gather in one room": 1.0× median, 1.2% of the reel
[instagram.com/p/Ddjnk6LFGYz](https://www.instagram.com/p/Ddjnk6LFGYz/)

The reel asked for the keyword «فريق» (team) and reached about 11× the usual reel; the carousel
came **three days later**. The cover is a full-height phone screenshot shrunk to a third of the
slide, its text unreadable, on a near-black background. Slides 2–6 are white icon diagrams
(laptop, cloud, document, people) that could illustrate any AI post. The template lived in a
200-word caption instead of on a slide someone could screenshot. **Broke: within 24 h; cover
readable at thumbnail size; real UI at legible scale; the template goes on a slide.**

### The icon-diagram run (four carousels in September): 0.7–1.3× median
"Gemini isn't only for text", "same model, why different results?", "let Claude press
Unsubscribe for you", "have a long voice note?". All on near-black, all built from generic line
icons and brand logos, no real screen, no number, no receipt. Save rates 0.8–1.2 %, the lowest
in the set. **Broke: show the real thing; no generic icons; a number or a receipt on the cover.**

### Receipts, but too small and with no reel behind it: "I let Claude talk to support: about $150 back, 3 times": 0.5× median
[instagram.com/p/DekD5ObjBPM](https://www.instagram.com/p/DekD5ObjBPM/)

The right instinct (real refund receipts, real policy text with the key clause highlighted, a
prompt to copy) let down by execution: first-person cover again; supporting lines set around
22 px at 1080 wide, unreadable on a phone; receipts at a third of the slide width; large empty
areas. It recapped three old reels instead of following a fresh one. **Broke: second-person cover;
minimum type size; crop the receipt to the number and make it big; ride a fresh reel.**

### The generic promise: "be ahead of 90% of people with AI, 3 simple steps": 0.3× median
[instagram.com/p/DaIoYc6iBMD](https://www.instagram.com/p/DaIoYc6iBMD/)

A chalkboard illustration, a "save this post" sticker, and a promise that names no tool, no task
and no result. **Broke: a specific outcome on the cover; no generic AI imagery.**

## The pattern in one table

| | Winners (3) | Losers |
|---|---|---|
| Reel on the same topic | the day before, with a comment keyword that promised this | none, or 3 days earlier, or a recap of old reels |
| Cover voice | "you" / imperative / question, the reader's result | "I let Claude…", the creator's face, the reel's story again |
| Cover payoff | free, no account, a job, a named tool | vague ("90% of people", "a word or a gesture") |
| Proof | real chart, real pricing table, a safety or caveat step | icons, mascots, AI-redrawn UI, unreadable screenshots |
| Last slide | one keyword or "save it and try it" | "the full template is in the caption" |

## Patterns from other creators

Short versions; sources and evidence type in [research.md](research.md) §3.

- **The copy-ready list.** On the biggest AI pages the top posts seen were lists you copy
  ("7 prompt symbols", "10 prompts"); a prompt list with a save line drew several times the likes
  of the same page's news carousel. The creator's version: the prompt or settings slide.
- **The TL;DR checklist before the CTA.** Ruben Hassid's 10-slide LinkedIn template ends hook →
  stakes → insight → 5 value slides → a checklist people screenshot → a question.
- **Value builds; it doesn't front-load.** Chris Do plans cover + eight content slides + CTA,
  puts the most valuable slide around 8–9, and splits any dense slide into several. A text-only
  carousel reached about 200K.
- **Every slide a standalone soundbite, plus one takeaway slide.** Vanessa Lau numbers each
  lesson so people comment their favourite, and keeps the design neutral and faceless so
  strangers reshare slides to their Stories.
- **Aim for the private forward.** Visual Capitalist designs for the WhatsApp/Slack send, with
  universal framing ("where does my country rank?") and the idea readable in one view.
- **Arabic, from an Arabic coach.** Ayoub Bellabes keeps to 10 slides or fewer, puts a small CTA
  around slide 4–5 because many readers never reach the end, and names the comparison format
  (beginner vs amateur vs pro) as the most reliable for reach.
- **Arabic AI page.** AI بالعربي runs a weekly roundup: a count in the title, dialect copy,
  English product names left in Latin, an open question at the end.
- **Counter-examples worth remembering.** Several million-follower AI educators win with reels
  or text posts and no carousels at all; the format is a complement to reels, not a substitute.
